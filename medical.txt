Cardiovascular Health:
 - High blood pressure (hypertension) is a common condition that increases the risk of heart disease and stroke.
 - Regular physical activity, a balanced diet, smoking cessation, and maintaining a healthy weight reduce cardiovascular risk.
 - High cholesterol can contribute to plaque buildup in arteries and increase the risk of heart attack and stroke.
 - Common risk factors for heart disease include diabetes, smoking, obesity, inactivity, and a family history of early heart disease.
 - Sudden chest pressure, pain radiating to the arm or jaw, sweating, and nausea can be warning signs of a heart attack.
 - Swelling in the legs, shortness of breath when lying flat, and rapid weight gain can be signs of fluid retention or heart failure.

Common Conditions and Symptoms:
 - Fever is a common sign of infection but may also result from inflammatory conditions.
 - Shortness of breath can indicate cardiac, pulmonary, or other systemic problems and should be evaluated if severe or sudden.
 - Chest pain can be a sign of myocardial ischemia (heart attack) and requires urgent medical attention when sudden or severe.
 - Cough, sore throat, and runny nose are often caused by viral upper respiratory infections.
 - Persistent vomiting or diarrhea can lead to dehydration, dizziness, and electrolyte imbalance.
 - Severe headache with neck stiffness, confusion, or neurologic symptoms needs urgent evaluation.
 - Painful urination, urinary frequency, and urgency can suggest a urinary tract infection.

Medications & Safety:
 - Always follow prescribed dosages; many medications have potentially serious interactions.
 - Do not stop or change prescription medications without consulting a healthcare professional.
 - Acetaminophen overdose can cause serious liver injury.
 - Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen can increase the risk of stomach irritation and kidney problems.
 - Antibiotics should be taken only when prescribed and should be completed as directed unless a clinician advises otherwise.
 - Sedating medications can increase fall risk, especially in older adults.

Preventive Care:
 - Vaccinations prevent serious infectious diseases; follow local public health guidance for schedules.
 - Routine screenings (blood pressure, cholesterol, cancer screening) depend on age, sex, and risk factors.
 - Hand hygiene, safe food handling, and staying home when sick can reduce the spread of infection.
 - Annual checkups can help identify risk factors before they become serious health problems.
 - Screening for depression, diabetes, and osteoporosis may be recommended based on age and risk profile.

Nutrition & Lifestyle:
 - A balanced diet with fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean protein, and limited processed foods supports health.
 - Adequate sleep, stress management, and regular exercise improve overall health and chronic disease outcomes.
 - Staying hydrated is important, especially during illness, hot weather, and exercise.
 - Limiting excess salt can help lower blood pressure in some people.
 - Tobacco cessation is one of the most effective steps for improving long-term health.
 - Alcohol misuse can contribute to liver disease, high blood pressure, and injury.

Emergency Guidance:
 - For life-threatening emergencies (e.g., severe chest pain, severe bleeding, sudden unconsciousness), call emergency services immediately.
 - Facial droop, arm weakness, and speech difficulty are possible stroke warning signs.
 - Severe allergic reactions can cause swelling of the face or throat, wheezing, and trouble breathing.
 - New seizures, severe confusion, or collapse require urgent medical attention.

Clinical Terms (short definitions):
 - Hypertension: persistently elevated arterial blood pressure.
 - Diabetes Mellitus: a group of metabolic diseases characterized by high blood sugar.
 - Myocardial Infarction: heart muscle damage due to inadequate blood supply.
 - Asthma: a chronic inflammatory airway condition that can cause wheezing and breathing difficulty.
 - Anemia: a reduced level of red blood cells or hemoglobin that can cause fatigue and pallor.
 - Infection: invasion of the body by harmful microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, or fungi.
 - Inflammation: the body's immune response to injury or infection, often causing redness, heat, swelling, and pain.

Respiratory Health:
 - Asthma symptoms may include wheezing, chest tightness, coughing, and shortness of breath.
 - Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) commonly causes progressive shortness of breath and chronic cough.
 - Sudden shortness of breath with chest pain can be a sign of pulmonary embolism and needs urgent care.

Digestive Health:
 - Abdominal pain can be caused by indigestion, infection, inflammation, constipation, or a surgical emergency.
 - Blood in the stool or black stools can indicate gastrointestinal bleeding.
 - Ongoing heartburn or reflux may improve with diet changes, avoiding late meals, and medical evaluation if persistent.

Mental Health:
 - Anxiety can cause excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, and sleep problems.
 - Depression may present with low mood, loss of interest, changes in appetite, fatigue, or hopelessness.
 - Thoughts of self-harm or suicide are a medical emergency and require immediate help.

Endocrine & Metabolic Health:
 - Diabetes can cause increased thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, and blurred vision.
 - Very high blood sugar may cause dehydration and confusion.
 - Low blood sugar can cause sweating, shakiness, hunger, and confusion.

Neurologic Health:
 - Migraine often causes throbbing headache, light sensitivity, and nausea.
 - Numbness, weakness, or trouble speaking may indicate a neurologic emergency.
 - Dizziness can be caused by dehydration, inner ear problems, medication effects, or low blood pressure.
